Adult Life of Shaheed Bhagat Singh

The Journey of a Young Revolutionary Who Dedicated His Life to India's Independence

1. Introduction to His Adult Life

Bhagat Singh was one of the most influential revolutionaries of India's freedom struggle. Although he lived for only 23 years, his courage, intellectual ability and commitment to independence made him an enduring national figure. His adult life was marked by political awareness, revolutionary activities, extensive reading and a strong desire to bring about social change.

He believed that freedom meant more than simply ending British rule. He wanted a society based on equality, justice and dignity. His ideas and actions inspired many young Indians to take part in the struggle for independence.

2. Influence of His Family and Childhood

Bhagat Singh was born into a family that supported India's freedom movement. His father, Kishan Singh, and his uncle, Ajit Singh, were associated with nationalist activities. Growing up in this environment exposed him to discussions about British rule and India's future.

The sacrifices made by freedom fighters deeply influenced his thinking. As he grew older, he developed his own political understanding and decided to devote himself to the cause of independence. His family's background helped shape his early interest in patriotism and public service.

3. Decision to Dedicate Himself to the Nation

As a young man, Bhagat Singh became increasingly involved in the freedom movement. He believed that young people had an important role in challenging injustice and creating awareness among the public.

Rather than following an ordinary career path, he devoted much of his time to revolutionary work, political discussions and reading. He travelled and worked with like-minded activists, seeking ways to oppose colonial rule and mobilise the youth.

4. Influence of the Jallianwala Bagh Massacre

The Jallianwala Bagh massacre of 13 April 1919 became one of the major events that affected the freedom movement. British troops fired on a gathering of people in Amritsar, causing many deaths and injuries.

The massacre intensified anger against British rule across India. It is widely remembered as an important event in the political atmosphere in which Bhagat Singh grew up. It strengthened the determination of many young Indians to seek independence and justice.

5. Participation in Revolutionary Organisations

Bhagat Singh became associated with revolutionary groups that opposed British rule. He joined the Hindustan Republican Association's revolutionary network and later became an important member of the Hindustan Socialist Republican Association (HSRA).

The organisation aimed to overthrow colonial rule and advocated a society free from exploitation. Bhagat Singh worked with revolutionaries such as Chandrashekhar Azad, Sukhdev Thapar, Shivaram Rajguru and others.

He also helped organise young people and promote political awareness. His work reflected the belief that the freedom struggle required both action and a clear understanding of social and political problems.

6. Formation of the Naujawan Bharat Sabha

In 1926, Bhagat Singh helped establish the Naujawan Bharat Sabha in Lahore. The organisation aimed to bring young people together, spread nationalist ideas and encourage opposition to colonial rule.

It also sought to reduce communal divisions and encourage unity among Indians. Bhagat Singh believed that young people should develop independent thinking, understand political issues and work together for a fairer society.

7. Simon Commission Protest and Lala Lajpat Rai

In 1928, the British government sent the Simon Commission to India to examine constitutional reforms. Because the commission had no Indian members, it faced widespread protests under the slogan "Simon Go Back."

During a protest in Lahore, Lala Lajpat Rai was injured in a police lathi charge. He later died on 17 November 1928. Bhagat Singh and other revolutionaries held the British authorities responsible and decided to avenge his death.

8. The Saunders Case

On 17 December 1928, Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and other revolutionaries were involved in the killing of British police officer John P. Saunders in Lahore. Their intended target had been James A. Scott, whom they blamed for the lathi charge on Lala Lajpat Rai, but Saunders was killed in a case of mistaken identity.

The incident made Bhagat Singh a major target of the British authorities. He escaped Lahore with assistance from fellow revolutionaries and continued his political activities.

9. Central Legislative Assembly Incident

On 8 April 1929, Bhagat Singh and Batukeshwar Dutt threw low-intensity bombs in the Central Legislative Assembly in Delhi to protest against repressive legislation. The act was intended to make a political statement rather than cause mass casualties.

They also distributed leaflets explaining their protest. Instead of escaping, both men remained at the scene and were arrested. Bhagat Singh used the ensuing legal proceedings to draw public attention to his political ideas and the freedom struggle.

10. Jail Life and Hunger Strike

During his imprisonment, Bhagat Singh and other prisoners protested against the unequal treatment of Indian political prisoners. They demanded better prison conditions and treatment comparable to that given to European prisoners.

Their hunger strike attracted widespread public attention. The struggle highlighted questions about prisoners' rights, dignity and equality. Jatin Das, who participated in the hunger strike, died in prison in September 1929 after a prolonged fast.

11. Bhagat Singh as a Reader and Writer

Bhagat Singh was not only known for revolutionary action; he was also a serious reader and writer. He studied history, politics, philosophy, economics and revolutionary movements around the world. Reading helped him develop a broader understanding of colonialism, inequality and social change.

His writings and letters reveal a thoughtful young man who questioned injustice and encouraged people to think critically. His essay "Why I Am an Atheist" is among his best-known writings. His intellectual legacy remains an important part of how he is remembered today.

12. His Political and Social Ideals

Bhagat Singh believed that independence should lead to a society in which people enjoyed equality and freedom from exploitation. He was influenced by socialist ideas and opposed social and economic injustice.

He also stressed the importance of communal harmony. He wanted people from different religious and social backgrounds to work together. For him, national freedom and social progress were closely connected.

13. Trial and Death Sentence

Bhagat Singh was tried in connection with the Saunders case. The proceedings became a major political event, attracting attention throughout India. Many people demanded leniency and appealed for his sentence to be changed.

Despite public appeals, Bhagat Singh, Shivaram Rajguru and Sukhdev Thapar were sentenced to death. Their case became a powerful symbol of resistance against British rule.

14. Martyrdom on 23 March 1931

Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were executed in Lahore Central Jail on 23 March 1931. Bhagat Singh was only 23 years old. The news of their execution caused widespread grief and anger across the country.

Their sacrifice strengthened the determination of many Indians to continue the struggle for independence. Every year, 23 March is observed as Shaheed Diwas in their memory.

15. Legacy and Inspiration

Bhagat Singh remains one of the most remembered figures of India's independence movement. His courage, commitment, writing and willingness to challenge injustice continue to inspire students and citizens.

His life teaches us the value of education, critical thinking, unity, courage and responsibility towards society. He is remembered not only for his sacrifice but also for his ideas about freedom, equality and justice.

Important Dates in His Adult Life

1926: Naujawan Bharat Sabha was established.

1928: Simon Commission protests and the Saunders case.

8 April 1929: Central Legislative Assembly incident.

1929: Prison hunger strike drew public attention.

23 March 1931: Bhagat Singh, Rajguru and Sukhdev were executed.

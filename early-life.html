Early Life of Bhagat Singh

The Childhood of a Great Revolutionary

1. Birth and Birthplace

Shaheed Bhagat Singh was born on 28 September 1907 in Banga village, Lyallpur district, Punjab, which is now in Pakistan. His birth took place during the period of British colonial rule in India.

His family had strong connections with India's independence movement. The atmosphere at home helped shape his interest in the country's struggle for freedom.

2. Family Background

Bhagat Singh's father was Sardar Kishan Singh and his mother was Vidyavati. He grew up in a patriotic family that discussed the condition of India under British rule.

His uncle Ajit Singh was also involved in anti-colonial activities. The experiences and sacrifices of family members influenced Bhagat Singh from an early age.

3. Childhood and Personality

Bhagat Singh grew up hearing about freedom, injustice and the efforts of people who wanted India to become independent. These experiences helped develop his interest in national affairs.

As he grew older, he became deeply interested in reading, history and political ideas. He wanted to understand why India was under British rule and how society could become more equal and just.

Did You Know?

Bhagat Singh was deeply affected by the Jallianwala Bagh massacre of 1919. The incident became one of the important influences on his growing opposition to British colonial rule.

4. The Influence of Jallianwala Bagh

The Jallianwala Bagh massacre took place in Amritsar on 13 April 1919. Bhagat Singh was around eleven years old at the time. The event left a deep impression on many Indians and strengthened the freedom movement.

The massacre contributed to Bhagat Singh's determination to learn more about the political situation and the struggle for independence.

5. His Early Interest in India's Freedom

Bhagat Singh's interest in India's freedom developed during his childhood. He learned about revolutionary figures and the sacrifices made by people who opposed British rule.

These early experiences helped shape the ideas that later guided his work as a revolutionary and his contribution to India's independence movement.

6. Beginning of His Journey

Bhagat Singh's childhood was the beginning of a life devoted to understanding injustice and seeking freedom. As a young student, he continued reading, learning and developing his political views.

His early life helps us understand how family influence, education and historical events contributed to the making of one of India's most remembered revolutionaries.
